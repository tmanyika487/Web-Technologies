# Prompt Log — About Me Section

This log documents the AI-assisted drafting of the About Me copy on my profile
page, as required for Part C of the assignment.

## 1. My Prompt

> **Context:** I'm a Computer Information Systems student building a
> one-page HTML5 personal profile/mini-portfolio for a web development
> course assignment. I'm still early in my studies — I know Python and I'm
> currently learning HTML, CSS, and Tailwind.
>
> **Goal:** Write an "About Me" section for my portfolio page that
> introduces who I am and what I'm interested in.
>
> **Constraints:** Keep it to 2 short paragraphs. Don't exaggerate my
> experience or make it sound like I'm already a professional developer —
> I'm a student. Avoid generic buzzwords like "passionate," "dynamic," or
> "results-driven." Keep the tone honest and conversational, not like a
> corporate LinkedIn summary.
>
> **Format:** Plain prose, no headings, no bullet points, ready to drop
> into an `<section>` element.

## 2. Raw AI Output (unedited)

> I am a highly motivated Computer Information Systems student with a
> genuine passion for technology and problem-solving. I am dedicated to
> mastering the art of web development, and I am constantly expanding my
> skill set across Python, HTML, CSS, and modern frameworks such as
> Tailwind CSS.
>
> I thrive in dynamic environments and am always eager to take on new
> challenges. My goal is to become a well-rounded developer who delivers
> clean, efficient, and impactful digital solutions, and I am excited to
> bring my enthusiasm and drive to every project I undertake.

## 3. My Final Edited Version

> I'm a Computer Information Systems student with a strong interest in
> coding and how software systems are put together. I enjoy the process of
> taking a problem apart, figuring out how the pieces should fit, and
> building something that actually works — not just in theory, but in a
> browser or on a screen someone else can use.
>
> Right now I'm focused on strengthening my fundamentals: writing clean,
> readable code, understanding how the web works under the hood (HTML,
> CSS, and eventually frameworks like Tailwind), and picking up good habits
> early — things like version control, accessibility, and documenting my
> own decisions — rather than bolting them on later.

## 4. Reflection

The AI's first draft ignored my own constraint against buzzwords —
"highly motivated," "passionate," "dynamic," and "thrive" all showed up
despite me explicitly asking to avoid that kind of language, and it made
me sound like a finished professional rather than a student who's still
learning. I rewrote it almost entirely in my own words, replacing the vague
claims ("deliver clean, efficient, impactful solutions") with something
specific and true to where I actually am right now — still building
fundamentals, not shipping polished products. I also folded in a mention of
the practical habits this course is teaching me (version control,
accessibility) since that's genuinely part of what I'm learning this
semester, not something the AI could have known to include.
